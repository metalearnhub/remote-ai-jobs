1. What "AI training" work actually is

AI labs improve their models with human expert judgment. Depending on the role, you might:

  • Evaluate AI answers in your field: is it correct, complete, safe and useful?
  • Write hard questions, reference answers and scoring rubrics that test what the model can't yet do.
  • Compare two responses and explain, in writing, which is better and why.
  • Test AI agents and apps in realistic workflows and report what breaks.

The common thread: clear, specific written reasoning from someone who has really done the work. Domain experience matters more than AI experience.

3. How the micro1 process works

  1. Profile and resume. They're the first thing the AI interviewer reads, and what it questions you on.
  2. Apply to one role. Choose a role that matches work you've actually done. Applying early helps.
  3. The AI interview (Zara). A recorded, conversational video interview you take when ready. Expect open-ended and scenario questions, plus a coding task for technical roles. Candidates commonly report 20–40 minutes.
  4. Result and review. Passing certifies you for the role; being placed on a project depends on client needs and can take days or weeks.
  5. Onboarding. Finish ID verification and onboarding steps the moment they're asked.

Process details come from micro1's public pages and candidate reports, and can change. Always follow your dashboard and emails.

4. Ten lessons for the AI interview

  1. Apply for the role you've actually done. Ask: "Could I talk about this for 20 minutes with specific examples?" If not, pick another role. This one lesson cost me two failed interviews.
  2. Tailor your resume, but don't overfit. Every line must be something you can explain in depth. Use the prompt below.
  3. Set up your profile before the interview. Highlight your strongest, most relevant skills and keep your availability current.
  4. Treat the job description as the syllabus. The 3–5 skills it repeats are what you'll be tested on. Learn the role's vocabulary.
  5. Take the free practice interview first, with the same room, camera and browser you'll use for the real one.
  6. Prepare 3–5 real stories in STAR form: Situation ~20%, Task ~10%, Action ~60%, Result ~10% with a number. Say "I", not "we".
  7. Go deep on the "how". Name the actual tools, steps, checks and numbers. High-level answers are the most common reason people fail.
  8. Be concise and structured. For multi-part questions say "First… second…". It's fine to say "Let me think for a second." Perfect English isn't required; clear specifics are.
  9. Set up like a proctored exam. Laptop, good light, quiet room, one screen, no other tabs or AI helpers. Don't try to game it.
  10. Follow through. Passed: finish verification fast. Didn't pass: request feedback, fix the gaps, and target a role closer to your experience.

5. Resume prompt (paste into Claude or any AI assistant)

You are an expert technical recruiter helping me prepare for an AI-led interview. The AI interviewer will read my resume and ask detailed deep-dive questions about it. List top strengths and biggest gaps. 2. Rewrite my resume so the experience that best fits this job is at the top. Use the job description's wording only where it truthfully describes what I've done. 3. Rules: do NOT invent jobs, tools, certifications or numbers. Don't keyword-stuff. Every bullet must be something I can explain with a real example. 4. List 8 deep-dive questions an AI interviewer would likely ask, and for each, the specific detail I should be ready to give.
